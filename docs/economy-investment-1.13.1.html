Economic investment corrections — 1.13.1

Port forecasts now account for the game's destination weights: destination level, an extra proximity weight, and an extra friendship weight. Both bonuses disappear below the short-range threshold. Equal-distance destinations preserve their observed order, including at the proximity cutoff. Own receipts, teammate receipts, rival spillover, and arrival delay use the same destination distribution.

Each sampled coast anchor has its own destination distribution; the forecast averages those distributions instead of mixing minimum distances from incompatible source locations. The forecast remains an advisory approximation. It uses at most eight sampled coast anchors, 32 owners, and 64 Port records, with at most 16 records per owner. Distances are geometric, not verified water paths, and the canonical-ocean filter does not establish access to every water component. Proximity rank is relative to the observed sample when those limits truncate the market. Unknown incoming traffic and the spawn rejection timer remain excluded.

These work limits apply after obtaining the shared adapter snapshots. The first player or owner-unit query in a tick can still materialize or filter a larger underlying list. That existing cached adapter cost is not bounded by the planner's sample size, so the regression checks do not establish that every game subsystem is free of long frames.

Port upgrade ranking now reuses a same-tick cached global TradeShip count. An unknown count leaves marginal upgrade output unproven. It no longer initiates a global unit enumeration to obtain that count. The legacy behavior with winFixes disabled retains its existing random sequence.

In normal Hard-or-higher play with an existing completed Port, the earlier naval lane usually warms the global TradeShip snapshot. Before the first Port, or when naval tracking is disabled, that count can remain unknown. Existing first-Port unlocking and construction priorities still apply; the forecast does not assume an empty world to promote a speculative Port investment.

The historical 1.13.0 validation remains in the original report. Version-specific validation for this correction is recorded separately to preserve that evidence.

Eight paired 6,000-tick matches retained identical final states and construction traces against 1.13.0: both versions won two and survived seven. This establishes regression coverage, without a measured win-rate gain. Bundle hashes, seeds, and outcomes are in the validation record. The full test suite, economic fixtures, and global quality checks passed.

A synthetic fixture calls the pinned engine's actual PortExecution.tradingPorts() with 13 destinations. It compares destination multiplicities, payout attribution, arrival delay, and the production investment decision. Equal-distance destinations straddle the fractional proximity cutoff. The teammate market selects Port; the equivalent rival market retains Factory. A second adversarial fixture keeps eight source locations separate: expected arrival is 1,365 ticks, while mixing minimum distances would incorrectly give 300. Work remains bounded at 512 distance evaluations and 64 friendship checks.
