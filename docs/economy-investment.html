Economy investment policy — 1.13.0

Economic construction must compare the additional value of the next City, Factory, or Port, its actual next-build cost, and the delay before it can produce. Existing emergency defense, the two-City bootstrap, troop-cap relief, construction legality, and resource reserves remain constraints on discretionary investment. A nearby teammate's treasury is not spendable gold.

Implemented comparison

With winFixes enabled, discretionary economic builds compare 300-tick and 6,000-tick forecasts with respective weights of 0.4 and 0.6. Construction and the geometric delay to the first Port arrival reduce the available earning window. Only positive weighted net forecasts reorder eligible alternatives; unknown or negative forecasts retain the existing order. Legendary's existing wait, mandatory, defense, and troop-capacity priorities remain authoritative. City troop capacity is handled independently: the model does not invent marginal cash from merely redirecting an existing self-owned train to a new City.

A per-tick snapshot inspects bounded samples of owners, completed stations, Factory levels, and ocean partners. Rail estimates use local station proximity and a conservative confidence discount, rather than claiming verified rail paths. Port estimates use observed partners on the canonical ocean, geometric distance, and outgoing traffic only. They omit unproven incoming traffic, actual water detours, the pity timer, and proximity/friendship destination bonuses. Congestion uses an already cached global TradeShip family count; when that count is unknown, no Port-income forecast is asserted. Ranking never initiates a world-wide ship enumeration, rail scan, or buildability request.

Own, teammate, and rival income estimates are recorded separately. The team objective adds the teammate's matching receipt once and discounts the rival's matching receipt by one half. This utility is not spendable gold: affordability and reserves always use the bot's own treasury. The coefficients are conservative heuristics, not a fitted probability of winning.

Once the existing placement-exhaustion guard admits an economic upgrade, the selector reuses the authoritative bounded upgrade probes. Within the same SAM-protection tier it compares the extra production per actual upgrade price. Factory comparisons include the change to all sampled own sources after the owner's completed Factory-level sum increases; each source's ten-tick cooldown is included. Port upgrades use a conservative one-level spawn-probability delta, and City upgrades compare the same added capacity at their actual prices. Disabling winFixes preserves the legacy random selection sequence.

Engine facts

  • Cities add troop capacity; they do not directly generate gold. Their commercial value requires a useful rail connection. Normal City and Factory construction takes 20 ticks; a Port takes 50 ticks. Instant-build games remove that delay.
  • Each Factory attempts train spawning by level, with a ten-tick cooldown. More owned Factory levels increase the spawn-rate denominator, so another source has diminishing returns. Trains need reachable City or Port destinations.
  • A train's first ten paying stops yield 10,000 gold for self trade, 25,000 for team or other players, or 35,000 for allies, before player multipliers. External stops also pay the station owner. Repeated stations in one rail cluster are not independent new markets.
  • Sea trade needs a tradeable foreign Port on the same water component. Own Ports alone do not form a sea-trade market. An arriving ship pays both endpoint owners; distance, partner levels, spawn rejections, and global ship congestion affect actual returns.
  • Port and Factory new-build prices share one level-based ladder. City prices have their own ladder. The worker's buildable descriptor remains authoritative at actuation.

Reproduce validation

The final eight-pair portfolio preserved all eight final states and construction traces: both versions won two matches and survived seven. Five matches per version remained unfinished at the tick limit. There is no measured win-rate gain in this portfolio. A separate full-decision synthetic case changes the choice from Factory to Port when an observed ocean partner is a teammate, and retains Factory for the equivalent rival market. The reproducibility record, bundle hashes, seeds, and paired metrics are saved in economy-investment-validation.json.

The final full test suite, global quality checks, TypeScript check, changed-file ESLint, and generated-artifact parity check passed. Deterministic performance tests cap local-distance work at 1,024 comparisons and Port-distance work at 512 comparisons, reuse the snapshot within a tick, and reject new worker, rail-index, or global ship-materialization requests. Candidate selection admits at most three productive types. These bounds cover the new planner's work; they do not claim that every existing game subsystem has zero long frames.
